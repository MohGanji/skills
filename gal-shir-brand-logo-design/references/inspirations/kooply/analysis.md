# Kooply — reverse-engineered (blind 3-agent method)

*Produced by **three independent, blind investigations** that never saw each other's work
— a brand-truth reader (the brand's own site), a Gal-Shir-commentary reader, and a pure
visual reader who deconstructed the actual logo **with no brand context** — then a
consolidation testing whether they align. Method note:
[../../gal-shir-principles.md](../../gal-shir-principles.md).*

- **Alignment (blind read vs. brand truth vs. Gal's intent):** **strong**
- **Idea move:** Native-grammar borrow + multiplied module: adopt the platform's own visual language (the mobile app-icon squircle grid) as the logo, then multiply and vary the module (four colored tiles, upright vs. diagonal glowing sparks) so one simple system simultaneously encodes play, creation, community/diversity, and motion — with the gutter's negative-space plus doing the "combine/connect" work for free.

## 1. Brand truth (blind)

Kooply (kooply.com) is an Israeli mobile-gaming startup (founded 2021; CEO Ido Yablonka) built as a reaction to a broken mobile-games market — "more than 99% of the mobile games will have between 50 and 100 downloads," and even good games "were never in the game... to succeed" because of distribution and monetization barriers. Kooply's answer is a **mobile-native game-CREATION platform** that removes those barriers: "we take on the development, the assets and the distribution so that you can focus on that vision."

- **Identity:** a mobile-first game-creation platform and community brand — *not* a game studio. Platform tagline **"Play, Dream, Create."**; flagship **Kooply Run** ("Run. Create. Compete.") with a built-in in-app level editor.
- **Audience:** three interlocking groups in one mobile-centric community — **Players**, **Creators** (everyday people, enabled by no-code editors + generative AI), and **Developers** (who go deeper via scripting).
- **Values:** creative empowerment, accessibility/democratization, community & connection, fairness/meritocracy ("reach the audience they deserve"), fun & imagination.
- **Personality:** playful, imaginative, optimistic; empowering and inclusive; a confident reformer, but approachable and community-minded rather than corporate.
- **Credibility:** $18M seed co-led by TPY Capital and Microsoft's M12, plus Playtika, Aleph, Samsung Next; leadership from CrazyLabs and Overwolf.

## 3. The mark, read blind

An analyst who never knew the brand described (confidence: **high**):

- A compact **2x2 grid of four rounded-square (squircle) tiles** forming a badge, each a different saturated color — orange (TL), red (TR), blue (BL), magenta (BR).
- Inside each tile, a **white pill/capsule**; the two left pills are upright, the two right are rotated ~45 degrees with a small circular hole/dot near one end — reading as "keys, game pieces, or tilted controllers." Together they create a **pinwheel/dynamic tension against the static grid**.
- The gutter reads as a clean **plus/cross (+)** in negative space — "connection, addition, modular app-grid or building-block feel."
- Wordmark **"KOOPLY"**: geometric, monoline, fully rounded terminals, circular O's, on a left-to-right **warm-to-cool gradient** tying back to the four tile colors.

Independent conclusion: *"A playful modular toolkit or platform: a grid of colorful apps, tiles, or game pieces suggesting creativity, play, and combining building blocks... a **mobile app-launcher / folder** metaphor, implying a suite of tools or mini-apps you play and create with."* Values read as *"playful, friendly, energetic, creative; approachable and non-corporate... diversity, fun, inclusivity, imagination... youthful, optimistic, maker-friendly, tech-forward but light-hearted."*

*(Note from the actual asset: the white "pills" are in fact **glowing light-trails with a bright bulb** at one end — a spark igniting / an idea coming to life — a detail the flat blind read slightly under-powered but did not misread.)*

## 4. Consolidation — do they align?

**Alignment: STRONG on the visible layer; SILENT on the intent layer.**

The blind read [C] recovered the heart of the brand truth [A] with no knowledge of Kooply:

| Brand truth [A] | Blind read [C] recovered it as |
|---|---|
| Mobile-native platform | "mobile app-launcher / folder metaphor" |
| Game **creation**, not a studio | "toolkit or platform... you play and **create** with" |
| Modular UGC, combine building blocks | "combining building blocks," negative-space **plus** = "addition, connection" |
| Play / fun / gaming | "game pieces... controllers... play... gaming" |
| Community, diversity, inclusivity, "audience they deserve" | rainbow palette = "diversity, fun, **inclusivity**" |
| "Play, Dream, Create" personality | "playful, friendly, energetic, creative... optimistic, maker-friendly" |

All four pillars — **Play, Create, Community/inclusivity, Mobile** — surfaced independently. That is a mark doing its job: a stranger read the strategy off the pixels.

**Divergences (all minor, none contradictory):**
1. **The intent layer is empty.** [B] gives no rationale, so we can prove the mark *fits* the brand and that the blind read *recovered* the fit — but not that any of this was Gal's stated argument.
2. **Spark vs. key.** [C] read the interior shapes as neutral "pills/keys/tokens." The real render shows **glowing sparks / light-trails** — closer to "an idea igniting" (Dream, Create) than inert keys. The top-line read was still right; the "spark" nuance actually *tightens* the fit.
3. **Kid-friendly vs. maker.** [C] leaned slightly "kid-friendly"; [A]'s user is the adult everyday creator/developer. The palette is playful, but the audience is a maker — a small over-read, not a miss.

## 5. Reverse-engineered process & lesson

**Likely path from brief to mark:**
1. Anchor on "Play, Dream, Create" and the brief's core tension: this is a **platform/community**, not a game or a mascot — so the mark must read as a **system**.
2. Borrow the platform's **native visual grammar**: the mobile **app-icon squircle grid**. A 2x2 of squircles instantly says "apps / games / launcher" — the load-bearing decision.
3. Encode "many creators, one community, inclusivity" through **count + color**: four tiles, four saturated hues (a rainbow that does diversity/fun/optimism work and yields four brand colors).
4. Inject "Dream / Create / spark" into each tile with a **white glowing bulb + trail** — an idea igniting, a game coming alive.
5. Break symmetry on purpose — left column upright, right column diagonal — so the four sparks form a **pinwheel**: motion, energy, a nod to the endless-runner flagship.
6. Let the gutter's **negative-space plus** carry "combine / connect / add" for free — the modular UGC idea at zero added complexity.
7. Unify the plurality into **one gradient wordmark** with rounded, circular, toy-like letterforms — friendly, not corporate.
8. Render glossy/glowing for an **arcade/candy** feel.

**Idea move:** *Native-grammar borrow + multiplied module* — adopt the platform's own visual language (the app-icon grid) as the logo, then multiply and vary one module so a single simple system carries play, creation, community, and motion at once.

**Lesson for designing in his style:** When the brand *is* a platform, make the logo speak the platform's native grammar — Kooply lives on the phone and is about a library of games, so the mark literally *is* an app grid, instantly legible because the audience reads that grammar daily. Then earn many meanings from **one** system instead of stacking symbols: multiply a module (four tiles), and let color = community/inclusivity, glowing sparks = "create," upright-vs-diagonal = motion, and negative space = "combine" — each meaning nearly free. Unify the plurality back into a soft, rounded gradient wordmark so it reads friendly, not corporate. And note the discipline forced by [B]: Gal ships marks with **no public rationale**, so the mark has to be **self-explaining** — the meaning must survive with zero caption, because there won't be one. The proof it worked: a blind viewer recovered the strategy without a single word.
