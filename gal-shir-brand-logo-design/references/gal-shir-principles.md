# Gal Shir — brand & logo design principles

Distilled from Gal Shir's writing, talks, courses (the "60 Tips for Logo Design"
e-book), his Lemonade brand work, and his public logo/identity work. This is the
*concept brain* of the skill — the taste and method the agent brings before a single
vector is drawn. The `engine/logokit` SDK is only the hands; this file is the eye.

> **The bar (Gal's own words, on an AI that designed a logo):** "It understood the
> brand story, values, audience, strategy, and turned all of it into a smart, minimal
> symbol. A genuinely brilliant concept… And it didn't just nail the idea. It executed
> the design pixel-perfectly." That is the two-part standard: **brilliant concept**
> AND **pixel-perfect execution**. Miss either and it isn't done.

## Who he is (context for the voice)

Tel Aviv–based multidisciplinary designer/illustrator, 15+ years. Founding-team brand
designer at **Lemonade** (insurance), where he built a warm, illustration-led identity
anchored on an ownable "Lemonade Pink" (#ff0083) and extended it into a character
system (the Maya chatbot mascot). Founder of **Color Hunt**. Sells premium bespoke
identity: *one* hand-crafted logo, no templates, no stock type, no revisions —
"one single logo hand-crafted to capture the essence of your brand."

## Core principles

1. **Story before form.** Every mark starts from a concept and a story in the head,
   then a quick sketch — never decoration-first. "My process usually starts with a
   concept in my head that I try to translate into a quick sketch."
2. **Compress the whole brand into ONE smart, minimal symbol** — a *concept*, not an
   ornament. The symbol should encode the brand story, values, audience, and strategy
   at once.
3. **Minimalism is an outcome, not a starting style.** "Cleaning, simplifying, and
   polishing a sketch to a pixel-perfect logo." You sketch broad, then reduce. Delete
   every meaningless shape.
4. **A logo must look great AND hold real value.** "A logo is the most valuable asset
   for any brand." Beauty without meaning fails; meaning without craft fails.
5. **Composition first, colour second.** "The first thing I keep in mind… is the
   composition." Then: "I spend hours adjusting and tweaking my colors to achieve the
   right atmosphere."
6. **Colour is contextual fit, not personal taste.** "When a specific color suits
   better in the composition… we consider it a good color." Treat every tool neutrally,
   "like a carpenter that loves all his tools."
7. **Custom everything.** Shapes and lettering are built from scratch to guarantee
   uniqueness — "built by custom shapes and lettering created from scratch." No
   templates, no stock typefaces left untouched.
8. **Humanize cold categories.** Warm, playful illustration makes hard/technical
   brands approachable (how he made insurance feel human at Lemonade).
9. **Taste = experience + intuition + reference.** "All three cores… are necessary."
   To create well you must consume well: study great marks daily before making.

## Visual grammar (what makes a mark read as his)

These are constraints, not decoration. Apply them by default; break one only with a
reason.

- **Tiny primitive vocabulary.** Build symbols from circles, arcs, and hearts, snapped
  to an explicit **geometric grid**. Assemble complexity by *repetition* (modular
  circle-packing — e.g. a mane or cloud from many identical tangent circles).
- **Golden-ratio / Fibonacci armature for curves.** Wave and motion marks are built on
  a golden spiral, radii stepping by φ. Use it when the concept involves flow/motion.
- **One uniform monoline weight.** Thick, even stroke throughout. No tapering, no
  mixed weights.
- **Rounded terminals and joins everywhere.** Sharp corners appear *only* when a single
  point IS the concept (a star tip, a beak, a wave crest).
- **Exactly ONE saturated brand hue + black/white.** Never more than one saturated
  colour per identity. 2–3 colours total, max.
- **One hidden second reading in negative space** — the signature "aha" (a bird cut
  into a letter, a beak from a triangle of empty space). Aim for one, never force two.
- **Custom wordmark tuned to the mark.** Rounded geometric lowercase sans for tech;
  bold rounded uppercase for mascots; script for playful/seasonal. Give it **one**
  deliberate letter twist that enacts the concept — no more.
- **Publish the construction.** Show the grid / golden armature beside the finished
  mark, plus mono + colour + app-icon lockups. Rigor is part of the deliverable.

## The method (brief → symbol)

An ordered, repeatable path — but stay concept-first, not mechanical. Gal explicitly
uses *no* rigid template; every logo is hand-crafted. Use these as stages, not a form.

1. **PREPARE** — what must this logo *do* for the brand? Its role as the core asset.
2. **RESEARCH** — extract product, audience, competitors, strategy, mission, values in
   one kickoff. (See the intake questions below.)
3. **SKETCH** — generate concepts/associations; mine the subject's shape, material, or
   function for a narrative/entertaining angle. Diverge widely.
4. **POLISH** — reduce to primitives; exploit positive *and* negative space; delete
   meaningless shapes; snap to the grid / golden armature. Converge to one pixel-perfect
   mark.
5. **CHARACTERIZE** — colour, shape, and stroke weight set personality (one saturated
   hue + mono, uniform weight, rounded terminals).
6. **LETTER** — draw the custom wordmark to match, with at most one concept twist.
7. **TEST** — legibility and consistency at every size/context: favicon, app icon,
   print, tiny and huge.
8. **PRESENT** — show realistic applications + construction grid + guidelines so the
   mark is undeniable.

### Intake questions (the RESEARCH kickoff)

Ask these before designing (infer from context if not answerable):

- What does the product do, in one sentence? What's the category?
- Who is the audience, and how should they *feel* about the brand?
- What are the 3 core brand values? The mission?
- Who are the competitors, and what do their logos look like (so we differ)?
- What's the brand's personality (playful ↔ serious, warm ↔ technical)?
- Any object, metaphor, letter, or story the brand is naturally tied to?
- Where will the mark live most (app icon, storefront, merch, docs)?

## Evaluation gate (judge before shipping)

A mark is done only when it passes all of these. Be your own harshest critic; iterate.

- **Conceptually smart** — encodes story/values/audience/strategy in one idea, not
  decoration.
- **Minimal** — fewest primitives; nothing meaningless remains.
- **Unique** — custom shapes + lettering; not mistakable for a template or stock mark.
- **Scale-proof** — reads at favicon size *and* billboard size.
- **Right personality** — colour/shape/weight match the brand character.
- **Negative-space "aha"** — ideally one rewarding hidden reading.
- **Ownable colour** — one saturated hue + black/white.
- **Geometric rigor** — holds up on a grid / golden armature; uniform weight; rounded
  terminals.
- **Undeniable in application** — convincing on the app icon, the tee, the landing hero.

## Symbol → mascot extension

The minimal symbol's geometry is reusable DNA. To grow a mascot:

- **Reuse the DNA:** the same primitives, grid unit, stroke weight, and single hue that
  define the mark become the mascot's construction system.
- **Start from a story, not a pose:** "My animation process starts before the
  illustration step, when I first try to think of a story."
- **Build the character from the same modular circles/arcs** as the symbol, so mark and
  mascot visibly belong together.
- **Keep it warm and animation-ready:** rounded forms, uniform monoline, the one brand
  hue (use on-hue tints/shades for shading — never a second saturated colour).
- **Extend to applications** — tee, mug, favicon, app icon, software UI, landing, phone
  — each derived from the same DNA so the system reads as one brand.

## Notes / honesty

- Gal's process is deliberately **non-templated and intuition-driven**. Encode his
  stages and constraints, but leave room for concept-first exploration — don't apply
  the grammar as a checklist that produces generic geometric marks.
- The golden-ratio armature is confirmed on curved/motion marks specifically; it is not
  a universal requirement — use it where flow is the concept.
- Gal does not publicly work "in SVG" or use "less but better" language; SVG is *our*
  single-medium choice for portable, pixel-perfect output, not a claim about his tools.
