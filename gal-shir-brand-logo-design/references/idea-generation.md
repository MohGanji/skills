# Idea generation — how Gal Shir invents a concept

This is the part the SDK cannot do for you: **inventing the smart idea** before any
vector is drawn. The `logokit` engine executes; this file is how you get *something
worth executing*. Read it during the **Concept** phase, alongside
[`gal-shir-principles.md`](gal-shir-principles.md) and the reconstructed examples in
[`inspirations/`](inspirations/).

> The whole game: turn brand **story → values → audience → strategy** into **one smart,
> minimal symbol**. The idea is the concept that survives reduction to the fewest shapes
> and still says everything. Everything below is a way to find that idea.

## Mindset (before you generate anything)

1. **Story first, shape later.** Don't start from "what shape?" Start from the brand's
   *story* — what it does, who it's for, what's funny/true/emotional about it. "My
   process usually starts with a concept in my head, then a quick sketch." Concepts fall
   out of a narrative, not a shape list.
2. **Consume before you create.** Reference is a core of taste. Gal scrolls ~30 min of
   popular work every morning: *"if you want to become a good creator, you need to start
   with being a good consumer."* Pre-load associations so more are available when you
   diverge.
3. **Diverge rough and wide, in private.** Generate *many* concepts quickly and cheaply
   (he sketches by hand on a dot grid: *"pen and paper… engages a different part of the
   brain"*). Quantity first — judge later.

## Divergence: turn one brief into many concepts

Run the brief through these **multipliers** — each turns a single subject into several
candidate marks. Aim for 5+ distinct directions before you judge any of them.

- **Three-axis object interrogation.** Pick the core object and mine it on three axes:
  its **shape** (silhouette/geometry), its **material** (texture, how it's made), and
  its **function** (what it does). Each axis yields different marks from the same object.
- **Name-mining** — literal, phonetic, and hidden-letter (see moves below).
- **Object fusion**, **feature-as-meaning**, **negative-space reveal**, and
  **contrarian inversion** — each below.

Don't stop at the first good idea. The point of divergence is to have *options* to be
ruthless with.

## The idea moves (named, reusable)

Each move is a concrete way to get from brief to concept. Reach for several per project.
*(Examples are Gal's where confirmed; some are his self-initiated concepts — treat the
move as the lesson, not the exact artwork.)*

### Mining the subject
- **Story-first association mining.** Turn the brief into a narrative, then brainstorm
  every object/symbol/word the brand associates with; let the mark grow from the story.
  *Vetric (data platform): "diamonds, like data, are hidden and hard to collect,
  therefore precious" — the whole mark grew from that one sentence.*
- **Mine shape / material / function.** Interrogate the core object on all three axes.
  *Vetric mined the diamond's rarity (material) and facets (shape) to signal quality and
  security.*
- **Metaphor bridge (tangible for abstract).** For an abstract product (data, AI,
  software), find a concrete iconic object that shares its emotional properties and let
  it stand in. *Abstract data → a diamond.*

### Mining the name
- **Take the name literally.** Treat the name as the brief: pick the most disarming
  everyday object it evokes and build the whole sensory world from it, clashing with
  category norms. *Lemonade: the drink dictated hot-pink, sweet tone, and rounded
  illustration — the opposite of navy corporate insurance.*
- **Phonetic name-mining.** Say the name aloud; listen for a real word hiding inside it.
  *"Articka" contains "Arctic" → a polar animal + icy palette.*
- **Namesake animal as the mark.** If the name is/contains an animal, make the animal the
  mark, reduced to its single most recognizable gesture. *Swan → the S-curve of the neck
  (which can double as an "S").*

### Fusing and doubling
- **Object fusion.** Find two subjects that share a silhouette; collapse them into one
  form that reads as both. *Moonwave: the crescent IS the wave crest. Biscuit Bear: the
  round bear head IS the biscuit.*
- **Feature-as-meaning.** Take an object's defining part and make it carry the product's
  core action. *Pakatu: the kangaroo's pouch carries fans' ideas to creators.*
- **Hide a letter in a pictorial mark.** Sculpt the subject so part of its contour also
  forms the brand's initial. *Threads: a bird whose forked tail is the stem of a "t".*
- **Letter play / mirroring / lettermark fusion.** Build from the initial with custom
  letterforms; mirror/rotate/fuse letters so the typography enacts the name.
  *Flyp: the "y" is a flipped "l" — the name literally flips around a central axis.*
- **Negative-space letter reveal.** Cluster shapes so the *void* between them cuts out a
  letter/symbol, rewarding a second look. *X concept: four heart petals whose pinched gap
  forms a four-point X-star / verified sparkle.*

### Mining the product & the verb
- **Weaponize a single color as the logo.** Own a hue no competitor has, tie it to the
  name/story, codify it to an exact hex, and let color identify the brand even without the
  wordmark. *Lemonade Pink #FF0083 — deployed full-bleed, even made into a platform named
  after the hex.*
- **Mine the material of the product itself.** Build the mark from the raw thing the
  product is/sells. *Color Hunt: the mark is a stack of colour swatches — a palette.*
- **Encode the verb.** Let the mark *perform* the product's core action via motion/form,
  not sit as a static emblem. *Float: gradient + motion make it appear to rise.*

### Refining the idea
- **Contrarian reading / defy the cliché.** Name the obvious treatment, then invert it —
  the twist becomes the concept. *Swan: "contrarian elegance" — straight lines and sharp
  origami angles instead of soft natural curves, signalling an engineered AI tool.*
- **Simplification-as-discovery.** Treat reduction as *generative*, not cleanup: keep
  stripping the form to fewer, more precise shapes until a surprising essential mark
  emerges. The concept often appears only once the form is fully reduced.
- **Golden-ratio / Fibonacci construction.** Refine the reduced mark against an explicit
  golden-spiral / overlapping-circles grid to lock proportion and curve harmony. *Used
  on the Moonwave and Threads curves.* (In the SDK: `goldenSpiral()`, `grid()`.)

### For mascots / personas
- **Personify the brand by voice.** Instead of a drawn mascot, embody the brand in a
  named character/persona whose tone makes the company likable. *Lemonade's Maya — a
  funny, human onboarding bot, vs. the claims bot "AI Jim".*

## Selection: choose the ONE idea

Divergence is many; delivery is one. Gal narrows ruthlessly and ships a single option —
he keeps sketches private because showing the pile "can confuse clients… choosing the one
that isn't the best." Selection is authorship, not a vote. The winning idea:

1. **Carries the story** — encodes brand story/values/audience/strategy as one smart,
   minimal symbol (not decoration).
2. **Reads instantly** — simple and legible at a glance.
3. **Survives every size/format** — favicon to billboard, app icon to merch.
4. **Often rewards a second look** — a hidden dual meaning (but only one; don't force it).
5. **Survives reduction** — distill via the origami principle until only the essential
   precise shape remains; the concept that survives *is* the one that ships.

Then, and only then, hand it to `logokit` to execute pixel-perfectly.
